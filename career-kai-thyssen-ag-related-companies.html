Kai-Roger Schmidt · Earlier career

Thyssen AG & related companies

  • Senior Steel Trader · Thyssen Stahlunion GmbH · Düsseldorf, Germany · November 1996–March 1997. Consolidated international trading activities and addressed CIS trade-financing exposures.
  • General Manager · Thyssen Delegate Office Mexico · Monterrey, Mexico · April–November 1996. Assessed strategic mill alliances, relocated the trading office and negotiated acquisition and securitization arrangements.
  • Delegate General Manager & Senior Steel Trader · Thyssen Delegate Office China · Beijing · December 1995–April 1996. Managed contract execution and negotiations with customers and state authorities.
  • Senior Steel Trader · Thyssen Stahlhandel Berlin GmbH · Berlin, Germany · September 1993–December 1995. Supported integration of Metallurgiehandel and developed Eastern European sourcing.
  • Steel Trader, Senior Market Analyst & Co-founder · TSU / Stahl International · Düsseldorf · March 1991–September 1993.
  • Steel Trader · Thyssen Stahlunion / Profilstahl Trading · Düsseldorf · May 1990–March 1991. Expanded sourcing and marketing relationships for long and semi-finished products.
  • Assistant to the Managing Director · HABAG GmbH · Düsseldorf · September 1989–May 1990. Countertrade, barter, linkage and compensation transactions.
  • Executive Management Trainee · Thyssen AG · Duisburg, Germany · August 1988–August 1989. Rotations in plate sales, flat-products quality control, market research, pipe sales, engineering, finance and controlling, and shipping and chartering.

When the e-commerce market changed, Stahlschmidt Incorporated (SI) acquired KPMG’s rights in KeyCommodities.com and put the technology to work in physical trade. When Thyssen and later Olympic Steel exited particular U.S. trading activities, the family acquired interests and continued building the business. MAN’s international metals activities were consolidated into Ferrostaal Metals Group and later combined with CCC Steel. The Nippon trading partnership developed into the BridgeTrade venture, whose remaining interest MetalsBridge acquired in 2022. After Solar Trust of America’s original operating chapter ended, its name and brand were preserved and revived in 2021 to house the family’s significant solar investments. The enterprise continued through changing markets, ownership structures and technologies.
