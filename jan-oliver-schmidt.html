Consolidated biography

The full perspective.

Jan-Oliver Schmidt joined SI in September 2011, following his work at Hellmann Worldwide Logistics. His experience spans corporate management, international shipping, project cargo, logistics, supply chains, and information management. His specialist responsibilities have included oil and energy logistics, heavy-lift and out-of-gauge cargo, chartering, tender and vendor management, P&L, and e-procurement.

At Hellmann, he served as Vice President, Projects (Americas), leading a specialized division responsible for complex shipments of plant equipment and machinery for industrial construction and maintenance.

Jan began his logistics career with Thyssen Haniel Logistics in Germany, with appointments in Düsseldorf, Duisburg, Frankfurt, and Bremen, followed by extended postings in Spain, Syria, Lebanon, and Singapore.

When Thyssen Haniel Logistics became part of ABX LOGISTICS, he continued into country management in Malaysia and regional corporate product management across Asia-Pacific and later North America. This international experience informs his shipping, transportation, logistics, and technology responsibilities within the family’s business history.

He is a graduate of the University of Western Ontario and Huron University College. His professional memberships included the World Affairs Council in Houston, the German American Chamber of Commerce, and the Economic Roundtable of the German Consulate General in Houston.

When the e-commerce market changed, Stahlschmidt Incorporated (SI) acquired KPMG’s rights in KeyCommodities.com and put the technology to work in physical trade. When Thyssen and later Olympic Steel exited particular U.S. trading activities, the family acquired interests and continued building the business. MAN’s international metals activities were consolidated into Ferrostaal Metals Group and later combined with CCC Steel. The Nippon trading partnership developed into the BridgeTrade venture, whose remaining interest MetalsBridge acquired in 2022. After Solar Trust of America’s original operating chapter ended, its name and brand were preserved and revived in 2021 to house the family’s significant solar investments. The enterprise continued through changing markets, ownership structures and technologies.
